## My Go-To Spinach Salad
A regular meal, "pretty much perfect."
- Spinach
- Pecans
- Salmon or chicken
- Blackberries
- Roasted sweet potatoes (oil and pepper)
- Goat cheese
- Lemon maple tahini dressing

**Typical logged bowl (Sept–Oct 2026):** about 1 cup spinach, 3.5 oz grilled sockeye, 2 oz sweet potato with 1/2 tbsp olive oil, 1/2 cup broccoli, 1/4 cup quinoa, 1/2 cup berries, and dressing of 3/4 tbsp tahini, 1/2 tbsp maple and 1/4 tbsp lemon ≈ 500 kcal, 34 g protein, 47 g carbs, 23 g fat, 10 g fiber.

### Why it works (the formula)
Tender greens + toasted nut + lean protein + sweet fruit + roasted vegetables + tangy soft cheese + sweet-acidic dressing. Swap within any slot:
- **Greens:** arugula, baby kale, butter lettuce, spinach-arugula mix
- **Nuts/seeds:** candied or toasted walnuts, pistachios, almonds, pepitas, sunflower seeds
- **Protein:** salmon, chicken, shrimp, turkey, steak, hard-boiled eggs, chickpeas, lentils
- **Fruit:** blackberries, pears, Honeycrisp apples, figs, pomegranate, dried cherries or cranberries, strawberries, peaches
- **Roasted vegetables (a favorite part of any bowl):** sweet potatoes and potatoes most often; also butternut or delicata squash, beets, Brussels sprouts, broccoli, cauliflower, carrots, red onion, bell peppers, zucchini. Grains like farro or quinoa can fill this slot too.
- **Cheese:** goat cheese, feta, blue/gorgonzola, shaved parmesan, burrata
- **Dressing:** lemon maple tahini, balsamic, honey Dijon, apple cider maple, miso-ginger
- **Extras:** red onion or shallot for bite, avocado, mint or basil

### Roasting
My way: **just oil and pepper** on sweet potatoes or potatoes. Keep it simple and let the dressing do the heavy lifting.
- About 425°F, cubes in a single layer with space between them so they brown instead of steaming.
- Flip once halfway; roughly 25–35 min, depending on size.
- Roast a sheet pan or two at once and keep them in the fridge for quick bowls through the week.

### Variations to try
1. **Fall:** arugula, pear, roasted butternut, candied walnuts, gorgonzola, chicken, maple Dijon
2. **Beet & citrus:** spinach, roasted beets, orange, pistachios, goat cheese, salmon, citrus vinaigrette
3. **Harvest bowl:** kale, farro, apple, dried cranberries, pepitas, feta, turkey, lemon tahini
4. **Summer:** spinach, strawberries or peaches, almonds, burrata, grilled shrimp, balsamic

## One-Pot Gruyère Quinoa with Green Beans, Burst Tomatoes & Pecans
*Developed together; sized for a 12-cup Viking skillet-pot about 2/3 full. Serves 6 as a main.*

**Verdict after making it: missing flavor, needed much more acid.** The "next time" fixes below are built in but not yet tested.

- 3/4 cup pecans, chopped medium-coarse
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium red onion, finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 1/2 cups quinoa, rinsed and drained
- 1/3 cup dry white wine, plus a splash to finish
- 2 tbsp Dijon (doubled from the first try)
- 2 1/2 cups low-sodium broth, plus a splash
- 3/4 tsp salt
- 2 cups cherry tomatoes (1 cup whole, 1 cup halved raw for the end)
- 1 lb green beans, cut 1–2 inches
- 1 1/2 cups grated Gruyère
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- Zest and juice of 1 lemon
- 1 tsp fresh thyme (optional)

1. Preheat the empty stainless pot over medium 1–2 min. Toast pecans dry 3–4 min; set aside. (No wiping the pot.)
2. Add oil and onion; cook 5–6 min. Add garlic for 30 seconds.
3. Toast quinoa 1 min. Add wine; bubble 1–2 min, scraping.
4. Stir in Dijon, broth and salt. Boil, cover, lowest gentle simmer 4 min.
5. Press the whole tomatoes into the quinoa; cover 4 min.
6. Scatter green beans on top without stirring; cover 7 min until liquid is absorbed and tomatoes split. Splash broth if dry.
7. Off heat, rest covered 5 min.
8. Stir in Gruyère and pepper, crushing some tomatoes.
9. **Finish bright:** lemon zest and juice, a splash of wine, and the raw halved tomatoes. Taste for salt and acid; add more lemon until it pops.
10. Fold in pecans and thyme right before serving.

Lessons from making it:
- "One pot" means one pot: no wiping or extra pans.
- Don't cook maple on hot stainless; it scorches. Maple and tomatoes don't belong together anyway.
- Tahini and Gruyère muddy each other; pick one.
- Rich, mellow bases (cheese, quinoa, nuts, broth) need acid added at the end, not just cooked in.

Alternate version to try: the same quinoa and green bean base with halved raw tomatoes and a lemon-maple-Dijon tahini sauce instead of cheese (1/4 cup tahini, 2 tbsp lemon, 1 tbsp maple, 1 tsp Dijon, warm water to thin).
