## Baba Ganoush
*Source: Cookie and Kate, "Epic Baba Ganoush." About 1 3/4 cups (serves 4–6 as a dip).*
- 2 lb Italian eggplants (2 small-to-medium, better than 1 large)
- 2 medium garlic cloves, pressed or minced
- 2 tbsp lemon juice, plus more to taste
- 1/4 cup tahini
- 1/3 cup extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for brushing and garnish (can drop to 2–3 tbsp for a lighter dip)
- 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley, plus more for garnish
- 3/4 tsp salt, to taste
- 1/4 tsp ground cumin
- Pinch smoked paprika, for garnish

1. Oven to 450°F, rack in the upper third, parchment-lined rimmed sheet.
2. Halve eggplants lengthwise, brush cut sides with oil, roast cut side down 35–40 min until fully collapsed and tender.
3. Scoop flesh into a mesh strainer; discard skin. Let drain a few minutes, stirring to release moisture. Discard the liquid.
4. In a bowl, mash eggplant with garlic and lemon using a fork. Stir in tahini, then slowly drizzle in the oil while stirring until pale and creamy.
5. Stir in parsley, salt and cumin; adjust salt and lemon.
6. Top with a little oil, parsley and smoked paprika. Keeps about 4 days refrigerated.

## Kolokithokeftedes (Greek Zucchini Fritters with Feta)
*Source: Eating European. Serves 6.*
- 4 medium zucchini, shredded
- 1/2 medium red onion, chopped
- 3 tbsp fresh dill, chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh mint, chopped
- 2 tbsp chives or scallions, chopped
- 1 cup crumbled feta
- 1 1/2 cups flour (self-rising best; all-purpose works)
- 2 eggs, beaten
- About 4 tbsp oil for frying

1. Salt the shredded zucchini and let sit 30 min.
2. Squeeze out as much liquid as possible (key to crispness).
3. Mix in onion, herbs and feta, then eggs, then flour.
4. Heat a heavy skillet (cast iron) and oil until very hot.
5. Drop tablespoons of batter without crowding; fry 2–3 min per side until golden.
6. Serve with tzatziki or Greek yogurt.

## Hummus
*Source: Inspired Taste, "Easy Hummus." Makes 1 1/2 cups.*
- 1 (15 oz) can chickpeas, drained (or 1 1/2 cups cooked)
- 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice (1 large lemon)
- 1/4 cup well-stirred tahini
- 1 small garlic clove, minced
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, plus more to serve
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- Salt to taste (about 1/2 tsp)
- 2–3 tbsp cold water or aquafaba
- Paprika, sumac or za'atar to serve

1. Process tahini and lemon juice alone about 1 minute until whipped.
2. Add oil, garlic, cumin and salt; process to combine.
3. Add half the chickpeas, process 1 minute, then the rest.
4. With the motor running, stream in cold water until smooth and fluffy.
5. Adjust salt and lemon; finish with a drizzle of oil and spice.

## Neufchâtel Crab-Dip Pita Pizza
Makes 2 personal pita pizzas.
- 2 pita breads
- 4 oz Neufchâtel, softened
- 1 tbsp mayonnaise
- 1 tsp lemon juice
- 1 tsp Worcestershire
- 1/2 tsp Old Bay, plus more to finish
- 1/4 tsp garlic powder
- 4–6 oz lump crabmeat, picked over and well drained
- 1/2 cup shredded mozzarella or Monterey Jack
- 1/4 cup shredded sharp cheddar
- 1 scallion, thinly sliced
- Hot sauce, optional
- Chopped parsley or chives, optional

1. Oven to 425°F. Bake pitas 2–3 min to dry and crisp.
2. Stir Neufchâtel, mayo, lemon, Worcestershire, Old Bay, garlic powder and hot sauce until smooth.
3. Gently fold in crab and half the scallion.
4. Spread on pitas, leaving a rim. Top with cheeses and remaining scallion.
5. Bake 7–10 min; optionally broil 30–60 seconds, watching closely.
6. Finish with Old Bay, lemon and herbs. Rest 2 min, slice.

Note: Neufchâtel is a lighter swap for cream cheese.

## Linguine with Clams
*Source: Once Upon a Chef. Serves 4–6. Soak clams 20 min–1 hr first. Logged at ~345 kcal per serving.*
- Kosher salt
- 1 lb linguine
- 6 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1/2 cup finely chopped shallots (2)
- 6 garlic cloves, coarsely chopped
- 1 cup dry white wine (Pinot Grigio, Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc; not "cooking wine")
- 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
- 2 lb littleneck clams (40–45), scrubbed
- 1/4 cup + 2 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped, divided
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 tsp lemon zest, plus more to taste
- 1 tbsp lemon juice, plus more to taste

Clam prep: discard any open clams that won't close when tapped, or with cracked shells. Soak in cool water 20 min–1 hr, then rinse and scrub.

1. Boil 4 qt water with 2 tbsp kosher salt. Cook linguine just shy of al dente; reserve 1/2 cup pasta water, drain (don't rinse).
2. Meanwhile, heat oil in a very large sauté pan over medium-high. Sauté shallots and garlic about 30 seconds until just golden.
3. Add wine, pepper flakes, 3/4 tsp kosher salt, clams and 1/4 cup parsley. Cover and simmer 6–8 min until clams open; discard unopened ones.
4. Add pasta (move clams to a plate if crowded). Toss over medium 1–2 min until it absorbs most of the sauce; splash in pasta water if dry.
5. Off heat, toss in butter, lemon zest, lemon juice and remaining parsley. Adjust salt and lemon.

## Crab Linguine in White Wine Garlic Sauce
*Source: Liz Della Croce. Serves 4.*
- 8 oz whole wheat linguine (reserve 1/2 cup cooking water)
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, grated
- Pinch chili flakes
- 1 lb precooked snow crab legs, cut into 3–4 inch pieces
- 1/2 cup dry white wine (Sauvignon Blanc) or chicken broth
- 1/4 cup lemon juice
- Salt and pepper
- Parsley and Parmigiano-Reggiano to garnish (optional)

1. Cook pasta; reserve 1/2 cup water.
2. Heat oil over medium in a deep skillet; sauté garlic 30 seconds.
3. Add chili flakes and crab, sauté 1 minute, deglaze with wine.
4. Raise to high, bring to a boil, cover and steam 2–3 min to heat crab through.
5. Toss in pasta, reserved water and lemon juice. Season and garnish.

## Japanese-Style Mapo Tofu (Mabo Dofu)
*Source: Sudachi Recipes. Serves 2–3 as a main.*

Pork:
- 100 g ground pork (or chicken or 50/50 pork-beef; not all beef)
- Pinch salt and pepper
- 1/2 onion, finely diced
- 1/2 tsp sake (or Shaoxing, dry sherry, dry white wine)
- 1/2 tsp Japanese soy sauce
- 1/2 tbsp yellow (awase) miso
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 1/2 tbsp chili bean sauce (toban djan); use 1/2–1 tsp for very mild
- 1 tsp grated garlic
- 300 g firm tofu, cut in 3/4-inch cubes

Broth:
- 200 ml chicken bouillon
- 1 tbsp sake
- 1/2 tsp Japanese soy sauce
- 1/2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- Slurry: 2 tsp cornstarch + 2 tbsp cold water
- 2 tsp chili oil (rayu), optional
- Green onions and chili threads to garnish

1. Brown pork with salt and pepper over medium.
2. Add onion; cook until slightly soft.
3. Stir in sake, soy and miso until the miso dissolves.
4. Push meat aside; in the empty space, sizzle sesame oil, chili bean sauce and garlic 30–60 seconds, then mix in.
5. Add bouillon and tofu; boil, then reduce to a simmer.
6. Add sake, soy, oyster sauce and pepper; shake the pan gently rather than stirring hard.
7. Simmer uncovered until reduced by about half.
8. Stir in slurry; once glossy and thickened, drizzle chili oil and turn off heat.
9. Garnish; serve over rice.

Tips: Adjust heat with the chili bean sauce and chili oil; if you cut chili bean sauce, add a little more miso to replace its salt. Optional finishes: sesame oil, sansho or Sichuan pepper, a splash of rice vinegar.

## Stuffed Pepper Casserole
*Source: Well Plated. Serves 6.*
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 lb ground turkey (or chicken or lean beef)
- 1 medium yellow onion
- 1 tbsp Italian seasoning
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 12 oz frozen chopped spinach, thawed and squeezed dry
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 1 green bell pepper, diced
- 2 cups water
- 1 (15 oz) can fire-roasted diced tomatoes with juices
- 1 (8 oz) can no-salt-added tomato sauce
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire
- 1 cup uncooked brown rice, rinsed
- 1/2 cup shredded cheddar
- 1/2 cup shredded pepper jack (or Monterey Jack/mozzarella)
- To serve: cilantro or parsley, Greek yogurt

1. In a Dutch oven over medium-high, cook turkey and onion in oil, breaking up meat, about 7 min.
2. Add seasonings and garlic for 1 min. Break up the spinach into the meat. Add peppers, water, tomatoes, sauce, Worcestershire and rice.
3. Boil, then cover and simmer 30 min. Stir, scraping the bottom, then simmer covered 10–15 min more until rice is tender.
4. Off heat, adjust seasoning (it thickens as it sits), top with cheese, cover and rest 10 min. Optionally broil briefly.

## School Lunch Cheese Pizza
*Source: Tasting History (from 1988 USDA school recipe). One half sheet pan, about 9 rectangles.*

Crust (pourable):
- 2 1/2 tsp (8 g) active dry yeast
- 1 2/3 cups (375 ml) warm water (110°F)
- 2 1/2 cups (320 g) all-purpose or bread flour
- 3/4 cup (55 g) instant nonfat dry milk
- 2 tbsp (25 g) sugar
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 1 1/2 tsp vegetable oil, plus more for the pan
- 1 1/2 tbsp (15 g) cornmeal

Topping:
- 1 tbsp + 2 tsp (12 g) minced dried onion
- 1/2 tsp garlic powder
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 2 tsp dried basil
- 2 tsp dried oregano
- 1 1/2 tsp dried marjoram
- 1/2 tsp dried thyme
- 1/2 cup + 2 tbsp (160 g) tomato paste
- 1 1/3 cups (330 ml) water
- 5 1/4 cups (565 g) shredded mozzarella

1. Oven to 475°F (425°F convection).
2. Dissolve yeast in warm water; rest 5 min until foamy.
3. Whisk flour, dry milk, sugar and salt in a stand mixer on low for 4 min.
4. Add yeast mixture and oil; mix on medium 10 min.
5. Oil a half sheet pan and sprinkle with cornmeal. Spread batter evenly (let it relax a few minutes if it resists).
6. Parbake 10 min (7 min convection).
7. Sauce: simmer seasonings, tomato paste and water about 10 min.
8. Keep oven at 475°F (450°F convection). Sprinkle 1 1/2 cups cheese on crust, spread sauce to the edges, top with remaining cheese.
9. Bake 10–15 min until browned on top. Cut into rectangles.

Ground beef version: replace half the cheese with just under 1 lb cooked ground beef.

## Chef Boyardee's Spaghetti
*Source: Tasting History (1930s Cleveland, from a family recipe). Serves about 3–4.*

**Not the canned product.** This is Ettore Boiardi's original restaurant-style meat sauce from his Cleveland restaurant: olive oil, a slow soffritto of onion and carrot, lean beef, strained tomatoes, mushrooms and fresh basil, over spaghetti tossed with butter and parmesan. No garlic or oregano.

- 2 lb canned peeled tomatoes (or fresh in season)
- 6 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 cup diced onion
- 1 1/2 tsp salt, divided
- 1/2 cup diced carrot
- 1 lb lean ground beef
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 5 medium basil leaves, sliced into strips
- 1/2 cup sliced mushrooms
- 8 oz dried spaghetti
- 2 tbsp butter
- 3 tbsp grated parmesan, plus more for garnish

1. Chop tomatoes and press through a food mill or sieve to separate juice and pulp from seeds; discard seeds.
2. Heat oil in a deep saucepan over medium. Cook onion with half the salt about 3 min without browning.
3. Add carrot; cook 5–6 min until onion is soft and translucent.
4. Add beef, remaining salt and pepper; cook 10–12 min until no longer pink.
5. Add strained tomatoes and mushrooms, bring to a boil, then simmer with lid ajar about 40 min until thickened.
6. Stir in basil; cook 5 min more.
7. Cook spaghetti, drain, return to pot and toss with butter and parmesan.
8. Ladle sauce over the pasta; top with more parmesan.

## Fettuccine Alfredo (Original, No Cream)
*Source: Tasting History. Works with fresh or dried store-bought fettuccine.*

Sauce, per 1/2 lb pasta:
- 1 cup (100 g) Parmigiano-Reggiano, freshly and finely grated (pre-grated won't emulsify)
- 4 tbsp (55 g) unsalted butter, room temperature

Homemade pasta (optional):
- 2 cups (230 g) 00 flour
- 2 cups (325 g) semolina
- 6 large eggs

Pasta: mix flours, make a well, work in eggs by hand, knead 7–8 min until smooth. Rest wrapped 30 min. Cut into 6 pieces, roll through a pasta machine with plenty of flour (fold and pass several times on widest setting, then thin to about setting 5–6), and cut into fettuccine.

1. Boil 3 qt salted water. Fresh pasta: 1–3 min; dried: per package.
2. Put butter in a room-temperature or slightly warm serving bowl.
3. Add pasta straight from the pot with about a third of the cheese; toss as the butter melts.
4. Keep adding cheese and splashes of pasta water, tossing about 2 min total until creamy.
5. Finish with more parmesan and black pepper.

## Roman Pork with Apples (Minutal Matianum)
*Source: Tasting History (from Apicius, 1st century).*

Roast pork (make ahead; you need 1 lb cooked):
- Pork shoulder, olive oil, salt, pepper, honey

Pork and apples:
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 3/4 lb ground pork or beef, rolled into 1-inch balls
- 1 cup chopped leek
- 1 cup chicken stock, plus more as needed
- 2 tsp garum (or fish sauce)
- Small handful chopped cilantro
- 1 lb roasted pork, cubed
- 1 lb sweet apples, cored, cut in 1-inch pieces

Sauce:
- 1 tsp cumin
- 2 tsp chopped cilantro
- 1 tbsp coriander seeds
- 2 tsp chopped mint
- 4 long peppers or 1 tbsp peppercorns
- 1/2 tsp asafoetida
- 1/4 cup white wine vinegar
- 2 tbsp honey
- 1 tsp garum
- 1/4 cup defrutum (saba, or reduced grape juice)
- 1/4 cup cooking liquid from the pot
- 2 tsp starch or cornflour, mixed with a little water

1. Roast: oven to 450°F. Brush pork with oil, salt, pepper and honey; roast 15 min, then 275°F about 1 hr per lb to at least 165°F. Rest 15 min; cube 1 lb.
2. Brown meatballs and leeks in oil over medium, 8–10 min.
3. Deglaze with stock and garum; add cilantro and pork cubes, with enough stock to cover the bottom. Simmer 15 min.
4. Add apples; cook 20 min more until meatballs are done.
5. Sauce: grind spices and herbs, combine with the liquids, bring to a gentle boil, thicken with the starch.
6. Serve pork and apples drizzled with sauce.

## Grilled Honey Mustard Chicken
*Source: Simply Scratch. Serves 4.*
- 4 boneless skinless chicken breasts
- 1/2 cup stone-ground mustard
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp paprika
- 1/4 tsp cayenne
- 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes
- 1/2 cup honey
- 2 garlic cloves, grated or pressed
- 2 tbsp lemon juice

1. Pound each breast in a partly open bag to about 1/2 inch.
2. Whisk mustard, salt, paprika, cayenne, pepper flakes, honey, garlic and lemon. Reserve 1/4 cup.
3. Toss chicken with the rest; marinate 30 min at room temp.
4. Grill over medium to medium-high 6–8 min per side until done.
5. Rest 5 min; spoon reserved sauce over before serving.

## Pad Thai
*Source: Tastes Better From Scratch. Serves 4. Logged at ~280 kcal per serving (as 12 servings).*
- 8 oz flat rice noodles
- 3 tbsp oil, divided
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 8 oz shrimp, chicken or extra-firm tofu, cut small
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 1 cup bean sprouts
- 1 red bell pepper, thinly sliced
- 3 green onions, chopped
- 1/2 cup dry-roasted peanuts
- 2 limes
- 1/2 cup cilantro, chopped

Sauce:
- 3 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 5 tbsp light brown sugar
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar (or tamarind paste)
- 1 tbsp sriracha, to taste
- 2 tbsp creamy peanut butter (optional)

1. Cook noodles just until tender; rinse in cold water.
2. Whisk sauce.
3. Heat 1 1/2 tbsp oil in a wok over medium-high; cook protein, garlic and pepper (shrimp 1–2 min per side, chicken 3–4 min).
4. Push aside, add a little more oil, scramble the eggs.
5. Add noodles, sauce, sprouts and most of the peanuts; toss.
6. Top with green onions, remaining peanuts, cilantro and lime wedges.

## Mango Chicken Curry
*Source: Get Inspired Everyday (Kari Peters). Serves 4.*
- 2 tbsp avocado or coconut oil, divided
- 1 lb boneless chicken thighs, 1/2-inch cubes
- 2 cups finely diced yellow onion
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tbsp minced ginger
- 1–2 tbsp red Thai curry paste (Mae Ploy preferred; Panang also works)
- 2 small kaffir lime leaves (or 1 tsp lime zest)
- 1 red bell pepper, 1/2-inch dice
- 1 (13.5 oz) can full-fat coconut milk
- 3 cups diced mango (2 cups for the sauce, 1 cup at the end; thawed frozen is fine)
- 1/2 cup chicken stock, optional to thin
- 4 cups roughly chopped spinach
- 1–2 tbsp lime juice
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- Sea salt
- To serve: jasmine or basmati rice, cilantro, lime wedges

1. Sear chicken in 1 tbsp oil until browned and cooked; set aside.
2. Add remaining oil, onion, garlic and ginger; cook on medium-low 4–5 min until soft.
3. Stir in curry paste briefly.
4. Blend coconut milk with 2 cups mango; pour in. Add lime leaves and bell pepper; boil, then simmer 8–10 min, adding stock if it gets thick.
5. Layer spinach on top, let wilt 1–2 min, stir in.
6. Stir in chicken, lime juice, fish sauce and remaining mango. Remove lime leaves; season.

Indian-inspired version: skip curry paste, lime leaves and fish sauce. Use 1 tsp turmeric, 1 1/2 tsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander and 1/2 tsp cardamom with the aromatics, plus another 1/2 tsp cardamom with the lime juice.

## Creamy Mushroom Wild Rice
*Source: The Salty Marshmallow. Serves 6.*

Ingredients: olive oil, onion, sliced mushrooms, minced garlic, salt, pepper, Italian seasoning, broth, wild rice blend, heavy cream, parmesan.

1. Heat oil in a large skillet over medium-high; cook onion and mushrooms until soft.
2. Add garlic for 1 minute; season with salt, pepper and Italian seasoning.
3. Add broth and rice, boil, then cover and simmer on low 15 min (true wild rice can take 45+ min; follow the package).
