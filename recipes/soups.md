## Manhattan Clam Chowder
*Source: Ask Chef Dennis.*
- 36 large clams
- 3 cups clam broth (bottled clam juice plus strained steaming liquid)
- 2 cups chicken stock
- 3 slices bacon, diced
- 2 large carrots, diced
- 4 stalks celery, diced
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 medium potatoes, diced (skin on or off)
- 16 oz canned diced tomatoes
- 1 tbsp fresh thyme
- Sea salt and black pepper
- 1 tbsp chopped parsley (optional)

1. Steam clams in 1 cup water, covered, over medium 8–10 min, only until they open.
2. Cool. Strain and save all liquid; supplement with bottled clam juice.
3. Remove meat, leaving the tough muscle on the shell. Chop small and refrigerate.
4. Render bacon over medium-low until a little crispy, 5–6 min.
5. Add onion, carrot and celery; cook over medium 7–10 min.
6. Add stock, clam juice, tomatoes, potatoes and thyme. Boil, then simmer on low 60 min. **Skim the oil off the top.**
7. Stir in clams, season, bring back to a boil, then simmer.
8. Add parsley; serve with crusty bread.

## New England Clam Chowder
*Source: Chef Billy Parisi. Makes about 20 servings; halve for a normal pot.*

Chowder:
- 1 lb thick-cut bacon, chopped
- 2 yellow onions, small diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 bunch celery, medium diced
- 1 1/4–1 1/2 cups flour
- 1/2 cup cream sherry
- 8 cups clam stock
- 8 cups chicken stock
- 40 oz chopped clams in juice
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 lb red potatoes, medium diced
- 2 lb russet potatoes, medium diced
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh thyme
- 2 cups heavy cream
- Sea salt, white pepper, Worcestershire and Tabasco to taste

Steamed clams for garnish:
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 15 littleneck clams
- 1 cup Chardonnay
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp chopped thyme
- 2 oz unsalted butter
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley
- Kosher salt and red pepper flakes

1. Cook bacon in a very large pot over medium until crisp. Remove bacon and about 1/2 cup of fat (keep separately).
2. Sweat onion, garlic and celery 5–6 min.
3. Stir in flour to make a roux.
4. Add sherry, canned clams, both stocks and bay leaves; bring to a boil until thick.
5. Add potatoes, thyme, cream, half the bacon, salt and pepper. Simmer on low 15–20 min until potatoes are tender.
6. Clams: sear in hot oil 1–2 min, add wine, garlic and thyme, cover 3–5 min until open. Finish with butter, parsley, salt and pepper flakes.
7. Serve chowder topped with clams, thyme, parsley, bacon, oyster crackers and pepper flakes.

Notes: If making ahead, add clams only in the last 10 min. If the cream breaks, whisk in a little roux over medium heat.

## Broccoli Apple Soup
*Source: Simply Recipes (adapted from Cooking the Nouvelle Cuisine in America). Serves 4–6.*
- 1 large bunch broccoli (about 3 heads with stems)
- 3 tbsp butter
- 1 cup yellow or white onion, thinly sliced or finely chopped
- 1 good cooking apple (non-tart: Jonagold, Golden Delicious, McIntosh), peeled, cored, chopped
- 3 cups chicken stock
- 1 cup unfiltered apple juice or cider (not hard cider)
- 4 sprigs lemon thyme or thyme
- 2 long strips lemon zest
- 1 tbsp chopped chives, to garnish

*Method reconstructed (the PDF cut off before it); matches published versions of this recipe:*
1. Separate florets; peel and chop the stems.
2. Melt butter over medium; cook onion and apple until soft, about 5–10 min.
3. Add broccoli, stock and cider. Tie the thyme and add with the lemon zest.
4. Boil, then cover and simmer about 20 min until broccoli is tender.
5. Remove thyme and lemon zest. Purée until smooth (blender no more than 1/3 full at a time, or an immersion blender).
6. Season and garnish with chives.

## Lighthouse Corn Chowder
*Source: Tasting History (Fannie Farmer, Boston, 1896). Lighter than modern chowder.*
- 1 quart (1 L) whole milk
- 3 tbsp fatty salt pork or bacon fat
- 1 onion, diced
- 4 cups Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled, 1/4-inch pieces (4–5 potatoes)
- 2 cups (475 ml) boiling water
- 3 cups canned sweet corn, drained (about 1 lb 4 oz)
- 3 tbsp (45 g) butter
- 2 tsp salt
- 3/4 tsp pepper
- 8 large plain dry crackers (not Ritz)

1. Scald milk to 180°F; set aside.
2. Render pork fat in a skillet over medium-high; cook onion 5 min until translucent and starting to brown.
3. Strain the fat into a pot; remove any fatty bits. Set onions aside.
4. Parboil potatoes in just enough water to cover, 5 min. Drain.
5. Add potatoes and boiling water to the pot with the fat; simmer until tender. Reduce to medium-low and add corn, milk and onions; bring slowly to a gentle boil.
6. Large crackers: break up, dip in cold milk 10 seconds.
7. At the boil, add salt, pepper and butter. Float crackers on top and simmer 10–15 min to thicken.

## Red Miso Ramen
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 1 yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 1/2 cup red miso (use generously)
- 1/4 cup tahini or Japanese sesame paste
- 2 tbsp mirin
- 8 cups water (or unsalted broth)
- 2 cups sliced shiitake (reserve 1 cup for topping)
- 2 packs fresh ramen noodles
- 2 cups baby spinach
- 2 green onions, thinly sliced
- 1 ripe avocado, thinly sliced
- Optional: chili oil, shichimi togarashi, nori, soft-boiled eggs, corn, protein

1. Sauté onion in sesame oil over medium 8–10 min; add mushrooms 2–3 min more.
2. Stir in miso and tahini 2–3 min. Deglaze with mirin, add water.
3. Simmer gently; don't boil hard after the miso goes in.
4. Cook noodles separately; drain.
5. Ladle broth over noodles; top with spinach, green onions and reserved mushrooms.
6. Finish with avocado and any optional toppings.

Tips: dashi or stock instead of water; ginger and garlic with the onions; soy or rice vinegar to balance; a splash of coconut milk for creaminess (don't boil after); toppings like crispy tofu, chicken, bamboo shoots, ground pork; sesame seeds or chili crisp.
