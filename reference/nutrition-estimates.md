# Nutrition Estimates from Photos

How to estimate calories and nutrition when Jesse sends a photo or description of a meal. **Base estimates on how he actually cooks, not generic or restaurant versions.** Read `reference/taste-profile.md` and the matching recipe in `recipes/` first.

## Portions come from the photo, not the old log
- The per-unit numbers below come from his Lose It food log (Jan 2021–Oct 2026, ~5,900 entries). **That log only covers periods when he was actively logging**, so it's not a full picture of how he usually eats.
- **Portion sizes drift.** Don't assume a photo matches logged amounts. Judge each portion from what's actually visible.
- Use the old log for **what** he eats and **how** he cooks it (ingredients, methods, which oil, which dressing, which salmon). Use it for **how much** only as a reference point, and say when the photo looks bigger or smaller than the logged norm.
- When a portion is hard to judge, give an honest range rather than defaulting to the logged size.

## How to estimate
1. **Identify the dish and check the recipes.** If it matches one of his recipes, use that recipe's ingredients and divide by servings, but size the serving from the photo. For bowls, use the component list below.
2. **List every visible component** and estimate each portion from the photo, using the hand first, then the plate, bowl and utensils, as scale. His bowls tend to have **many components**, so look for small amounts of several things.
3. **Apply his methods by default** unless the photo clearly shows otherwise:
   - **Salmon is wild sockeye, always cooked, grilled plain: no oil, no seasoning.** Don't add cooking fat or glaze calories to it.
   - Roasted sweet potatoes, potatoes and other veg: olive oil and pepper. **He uses more oil than you'd guess**, often the single biggest calorie swing. The oil is on the vegetables, not the fish. Glossy vegetables mean more oil.
   - Dressing is usually lemon maple tahini (see `recipes/sauces-and-dressings.md`); sometimes olive oil + red wine vinegar with hummus.
   - Minimal butter; often lighter or plant-based swaps (Just Egg, vegan mayo, plant-based cheese and ranch, fat-free ultra-filtered milk).
   - Full-fat on purpose, don't assume lightened: original Alfredo, New England chowder, peach pie and ice cream.
4. **Restaurant and takeout food:** he sometimes eats only half or a third of a restaurant portion. Estimate what he says he ate, or what's on the plate in the photo.
5. **Hidden calories are the biggest error**: olive oil, tahini/dressing, avocado, nuts, cheese. List these as their own components.

## Accuracy
Photo-based estimates are typically within about 20–30% at best; the hand in frame helps a lot. Oil and tahini are the hardest to see. A quick note ("1 tbsp oil," "4 oz salmon") tightens them further.
